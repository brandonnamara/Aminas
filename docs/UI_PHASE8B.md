# Aminas · Phase 8B — Full Monster Roster Polish

**Branch:** `ui8b-roster`, created from `ui8-monsters` (the approved Phase 8 work, `5e48592`). Production (`main`,
`0fc9e36`) and every earlier branch are untouched.

**Scope:** every monster that had not had the Phase 8 treatment: 58 forms in 20 lines (the table in
section 1). Umbrit (stage 1), Cannonis (stage 1) and the three Aimza forms keep their approved Phase 8 work
unchanged; their own clips, choreography and voices are untouched.

Review page: `docs/ui/phase8b-review.html`. Its media is in `docs/ui/screens8b/`: before/after pose sheets for every
line, in-game screenshots, animation videos and sound samples.

## 2. Models: the same blocks, now in moving parts
* **No block changed.** Every form keeps exactly the blocks it had in Phase 8: the same positions, colours and glow
  (the test compares a hash of every block with the Phase 8 build). Silhouettes, colours, 2D portraits, cards and
  island critters are therefore unchanged, and nothing needed re-baking.
* **Rigged while sculpted (`src/voxel-rig.js`, the species builders).** Each body plan (four-legged, primate,
  dragon, two-legged, and the one-off builders: sea serpent, shell beast, bird, construct, floating shell, rooted
  sprout, round body, pebble) now records its anatomy while it draws: which blocks are the head, jaw, neck, each
  leg, arm, wing, the tail, ears or crown, and where each joint, eye and mouth is. A finishing pass then:
  * joins decorations (tufts, flames, manes, crystals, leaves) to the part they grow from;
  * makes floating debris (stones, crystals, cards, wisps, ribbons) an "aura" part that drifts slowly around the
    creature, and rings a "halo";
  * gives any untagged block the part of its nearest neighbour;
  * splits jaws from heads;
  * adds eyelids in the hidden layer in front of every pair of eyes (they appear only while blinking, never in 2D).
* **One skinned mesh per creature (`src/3d/creature3d.js`).** Each creature is built as one skinned mesh (plus one
  for its glowing blocks) with a bone per part. Parts cost no extra draw calls, the outline follows the bones, and
  copies for battle re-bind their own bones. Largest form: 9,676 triangles (budget 20,000); build time on the
  desktop test machine 4–90 ms, then cached.
* **Terraflora I** gained the most: its eight roots now step as four legs, its leaf crown rustles and its two buds
  twitch on their own.

## 3. Animations (`src/3d/roster.js`)
**Contract (unchanged):** the same clip names and lengths; every clip except defeat returns to its start pose;
attacks reach the foe at 0.34 s and specials at 0.40 s, so battles take no longer.

* **Idle:** breathing, a wandering look, tail, wings and ears, and once per cycle the line's quirk (table above).
  It is sampled finely enough for quick quirks (a shake, a wag) to read as intended.
* **Blink:** an eyelid-only overlay, every 4–6 s depending on the line, with an occasional double blink.
* **Ready, attack, special:** a style per line (16 attack and 19 special styles in all), with anticipation before
  the strike and follow-through after it. Jaws open, tails counter-swing, wings flare, roots plant.
* **Reactions:** hit, heavy hit, heal and guard; eyelids squeeze shut on a hit.
* **Victory and defeat:** a style per line (17 victories, 13 defeats).
* **Move:** each creature's own gait (stride, bounce, sway and waddle from its build and temper).
* **Individual, not copied:** shared styles are performed in each creature's own way. Livelier lines move bigger
  and snappier; heavier and older forms reach each pose later and smaller. Start and end poses, and the battle
  timing, are unchanged. The test checks that no two of the 58 forms share an idle, gait, attack, special,
  hit reaction, victory or defeat.
* **Living details (as Phase 8):** glow that breathes, flares for specials and victories and dims on defeat; a
  ground light or pool in the line's colour; floating parts drifting slowly around the creature and answering each
  action (they draw in on a guard and flare out on a special). All of it stops with Reduce Motion.
* **Viewer:** each line opens at its own three-quarter angle in the details screen.

## 4. Evolution
* **Names** keep the convention, Monster / Monster II / Monster III; the data is unchanged.
* **Movement grows with the stage, as Aimza's does:** stage I is quicker and bouncier, looks around more and wags
  more; stage II is the line's baseline; stage III breathes deeper, carries more weight, reaches each pose later
  and holds it. In every full line the idle cycle lengthens from I to III.
* **Anatomy that a form gains moves at once:** Aquaflora II's legs, Lumi II's wings, Pyro-Core II's limbs, the
  orbiting stones of Gaiaroki, Terraflora III's crystals, Voltar III's cards and Zeep III's halo.
* **Voices deepen:** each stage calls lower than the one before, and the third form adds a body tone.
* **The evolution ceremony** reveals the new form playing its own victory with its voice.

## 6. Sounds (`src/audio.js`)
* **Original:** synthesized in code like the Phase 8 voices; no recordings, nothing to license.
* **A voice per line:** its own pitch, timbre, call shape (rising chirp, growl, wobble, song, bugle, cry, beep,
  chatter, giggle, tweet, servo) and its element's textures. Each form uses it at its own pitch.
* **Cues:** tap, idle, move, attack, special, impact, hurt, heal, guard, victory, defeat; in battle and in the
  details screen, as for the showcase monsters.
* **Balance:** the same bus, compressor and limits as Phase 8 (six at once, three for idle, tap and step sounds;
  never the same cue twice within 120 ms; silent when sound effects are off). Every cue of every voice was
  rendered: the loudest peaks at 0.25, and the median sits with the Phase 8 voices.

## 7. Developer panel
**Monster showcase** (Settings, tap the version line 7 times) now lists all 63 forms: the five Phase 8 forms first,
then the roster by line, with quick links. View opens any form's details screen (3D, clips, Special, sounds), owned
or not. Battle starts a practice battle against the same stage of a rival line. Nothing is saved or earned.

## Limitations
* **Same sculpts, new life.** The roster keeps its blocks exactly, so nothing can drift from the approved designs.
  They were not re-sculpted at higher detail as the three showcase monsters were; that would be a separate art
  pass, line by line.
* **Shared styles.** Some lines share an attack, victory or defeat style (for example the punch). No two forms move
  identically, but those are variations rather than unique choreography.
* **Small first forms.** Lumi I (a floating shell) and Pyro-Core I (a pebble with arms) have few parts to move; they
  rely on whole-body motion, wisps or arms, eyelids and effects.
* **No bloom**, as in Phase 8.
* **Not tested on a real iPhone.** Captures and measurements are from desktop software graphics.

## Testing on your iPhone
1. Open the dev preview. Settings → tap the version line 7 times → **Monster showcase**. Open a few forms from
   different lines (View) and play Attack, Special and Victory; tap the creature to hear it.
2. Start a few showcase battles (Battle) and use both moves.
3. Evolve a monster you own and watch the new form appear.
4. Check the sound balance against Umbrit, Cannonis and Aimza, and that everything is silent with sound effects
   off. Try Reduce Motion and Battery Saver.
