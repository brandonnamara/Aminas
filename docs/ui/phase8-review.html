Aminas · Phase 8 review

Three showcase monsters: Umbrit, Cannonis and the whole Aimza line, rebuilt in moving voxel parts with their own animations, move choreography and voices. Plus a prismatic gem icon and the fix for feeding resetting the details screen. Screenshots compare Phase 7 with Phase 8 from the same scripted scenes; tap any to see it full size.

The models

Each is rebuilt at higher detail in separately moving parts (ears, eyelids, jaws, heads, wings, tail, Cannonis’s cannon). The same model draws the 2D portraits and cards, so they changed too. Duskmantle, Eclipsar, Reefback and Shellfin, and every other monster, are untouched.

Umbrit — The Velvet Shadow

Back to its original starter design: a round velvet-indigo baby with pointed ears (pale inside), big friendly eyes with highlights, blush, a small smile, a pale belly, stubby feet, a glowing moon-crescent crest and a wisp tail that fades into violet light. In 3D its shadow pools under it.

Cannonis — The Living Artillery

From its card: a hunched shell beast whose dome is large stone and blue plates with water light in some seams, a banded living cannon with a glowing bore, a blue head under a stone brow with glowing eyes and a cream jaw, stout plated legs with pale claws.

Aimza I — The Awakening

From its card: a stocky white hatchling dragon with a big head, large cyan eyes, swept-back horns, bat wings with a few glowing membrane cells, a glowing chest core and a cyan tail tip. In 3D it stands on a glowing ring, as on the card.

Aimza II — The Second Head

Near-black with cyan circuitry: a second head on its own neck, larger wings whose membranes glow in pixel clusters, a glowing seam down the chest and neck, dorsal spikes.

Aimza III — The Third Head

Three heads (the centre one tallest), the largest wings, more spikes and glow. Each head has its own jaw, eyelids and mouth, so they can look, blink and strike on their own.

Animations

Every clip is their own; no two of the three share movement. Eyelids blink on an overlay that runs alongside everything else. Attacks reach the foe at 0.34 s and specials at 0.40 s, exactly as before, so battles take no longer. Rendered frame by frame from the game’s own clips.

Battle moves

Each of their moves has its own sequence, timed to the animation: shots leave from the real cannon muzzle or from each head’s mouth. Everything is built from cubes, scaled down with Battery saver and Reduce motion. Moves, power, targeting and cooldowns are unchanged.

  • Shade Nip: a coil, a hop-bite, two shadow slashes.
  • Gloom Veil: the crest flares, a shadow orb crosses, a veil of dusk spirals down around the foe.
  • Bubble Pop: a muzzle flash and a stream of bubbles as the barrel kicks back.
  • Tidal Surge: it rears with a roar, slams down, throws a wall of water along the ground and fires through it.
  • Echo Wave: one head draws back and sends widening rings of light (Aimza’s basic move, so no ultimate camera).
  • Hum: rings of light rise around Aimza with its eyes closed.
  • Double / Triple Echo: every head rears, then beams from each mouth converge on the foe, followed by rings and a shockwave.

Sounds

Original, synthesized in code: no recordings, nothing to license. Umbrit chirps, purrs and hisses; Cannonis rumbles, booms and splashes; Aimza speaks in digital chimes and echoes, one voice per head. They play on their own quieter bus through a compressor, at most six at once, never the same sound twice within 120 ms, and only when sound effects are on. Every other monster keeps the shared sounds.

Gem icon

The same readable silhouette, now a prismatic crystal: a pale crown with a sparkle, rainbow facets (rose, violet, cyan, mint, gold) that deepen towards the point, and a soft prismatic glow. On the top bar it glints briefly every 7 seconds; the glint is off with Reduce motion, Battery saver and while the island rests. The other currency icons are unchanged.

Feeding no longer resets the details screen

Before, feeding rebuilt the whole screen: the 3D viewer could restart and the tapped button vanished. Now the screen updates in place: the same viewer, tab, scroll position and selected Amina; level, experience and stats change immediately. The automated test feeds six times in a row and checks that every element is the same one each time.

Tests and performance

Tests: 596 of 596 passed. The new suite (38 checks) covers the models and parts, the animation contracts, the choreography of every move in battle, the sounds (every cue renders, none clips, settings respected), feeding six times without a reset, the gem, and that every other monster is unchanged. It also found a Phase 7 bug, now fixed: on the 2D battle stage (3D off), a super-effective hit froze the battle.

Performance, desktop software graphics at 844×390 @2×, Balanced, Island Boss room. Software rendering overstates graphics work; only the before/after difference means anything. On the iPhone a whole Phase 7 battle frame cost about 1–2 ms.

Limitations

Close to the cards, not copiesBuilt voxel by voxel in code from the reference art; fine painted details (individual scales, every glowing pixel) are simplified, and the sides and backs are interpreted.
No bloomThe cards’ glow halos come from a bloom effect the game does not have; adding it would change every creature and arena. Glow here is emissive voxels, a breathing pulse and ground light.
Aimza’s heads in 2DOn the side-on 2D portraits and cards the extra heads partly overlap; they read clearly in 3D.
More to drawMoving parts mean more draw calls (up to about 80 with effects and outlines in the heaviest pairing, against about 30 before). Fine for the phone’s headroom; worth confirming with the battle test.
Not tested on a real iPhoneAll captures and measurements here are from desktop software graphics.
